# After Stories — marketing guidelines

> **What we say, in every channel.** For Hermes and anyone else who writes a video, a reel, a
> carousel, a caption, a blog post, an ad or a store text for After Stories.
>
> **Status: proposal, 2026-11-05 (#81).** It binds once Robert merges it. The lines marked
> *(Robert)* in §11 are his to decide; until he does, the recommended choice stands.
>
> This file decides **the message and the facts**. `marketing.md` decides how videos are produced
> and scheduled, `prompt.md` how a blog post is built, `messaging.md` how the site's pages are
> structured, `positioning.md` why we chose this market. Where one of them disagrees with this
> file about what we say or what is true, **this file wins** (§10).

## 1. The problem this file fixes

People who see our content can't say what After Stories is (owner, 2026-11-05). Three causes:

1. **We never say what it is.** The site's headline, the App Store's first line and the end cards
   carry the tagline, which names no product. A caption as built (as-send-the-pics, 2026-11-03):
   *"Who in your group never sends the pics? / One stream for the whole event. / Wake up to the
   story."* Nothing in it says this is an app, or what it does. The clearest description we have
   is the first line of `llms.txt`, a file only crawlers read.
2. **We say a different thing every time.** Four series, twelve occasions and a dozen feature
   spotlights give seventy episodes in the plan, each with its own message. With a few hundred
   views per post, no message is seen twice. One message said seventy ways is remembered;
   seventy messages are not.
3. **Our facts drift.** Four files kept their own feature list, each checked on a different day.
   A writer who has to be truthful with sources that disagree gets vague to stay safe.

## 2. The message

Learn this section. Everything else in the file is how to use it.

### 2.1 What it is: one sentence, the same every time

| | English | Danish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full** (captions, blog, voice, replies) | After Stories is an app for one event with your group: a dinner, a party, a weekend away. Everyone votes on the plan, shares photos in one place, and saves them all with one tap the morning after. | After Stories er en app til ét event med din gruppe: en middag, en fest, en weekendtur. Alle stemmer om planen, deler billeder ét sted og gemmer dem alle med ét tryk morgenen efter. |
| **Short** (on screen, ≤ 12 words) | The app for one event with your group. | Appen til ét event med din gruppe. |

- The three examples may be swapped for the piece's own occasion (*a birthday, the kids' match,
  the office party*). Everything else in the sentence stays word for word.
- "Event" is the app's own noun, but alone it sounds like a conference. The first time it
  appears in a piece, examples follow it.

### 2.2 The promise: the line that leads

> **Plan it together. Everyone gets the photos.**
> *Planlæg det sammen. Alle får billederne.*

It names the two things people get, in words they use. The occasion pages already work this way
(*"Plan the birthday together. Everyone gets the photos."*); this is the same line for every
occasion.

The tagline ***Plan it together. Live it together. Wake up to the story.*** stays as the brand's
sign-off. It never stands alone and never does the explaining: it may appear only in a piece that
has already said what the app is.

### 2.3 The three proofs: why it's true

Always these three, in these words. **One piece of content uses one proof.**

| # | Proof | The situation it answers | The line | What to show (the real app) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The plan stays on top** | "wait, what time was it again?" | What's decided stays at the top of the event: the day, the place, who brings what. Nobody scrolls back. | A vote in the chat, then *Event details* at the top |
| 2 | **Everyone's photos in one place** | "can someone send the pics??" | Everyone's photos land in the event. The morning after, you save them all with one tap. | The morning-after summary, then *Save all photos* |
| 3 | **Then it's gone** | The group chat that never dies; the photo that resurfaces | 48 hours after it ends, the event is deleted for everyone. Save the photos first. | The saved photos, then *Deleted in … hours* |

Danish: **Planen står øverst** · **Alles billeder ét sted** · **Så er det væk**.

Proof 2 gets the most pieces (about half): it is the most visual, and it is what the name means.
Proof 3 always shows saving before deleting (§4, rule 8).

Everything else the app does (templates, the cover, likes, the QR code, places, the web app) is a
**supporting fact**: it may appear inside a piece, but it is never the piece's message. The one
exception is the carpool, which is proof 1 for parents (§6).

### 2.4 Why not just the group chat? One answer

> **Your group chat is for everything, forever. After Stories is for this one event, and then
> it's gone.**
> *Gruppechatten er til alt, for altid. After Stories er til dette ene event, og så er det væk.*

The villain is still the group chat, never a competitor by name. But After Stories has a chat
too, so "group chats are bad" alone confuses people. This line is the difference; use it whenever
a piece attacks the group chat.

### 2.5 The name

"After" is the morning after; the "story" is what everyone gets then. The word *story* never
stands unexplained. Once per piece it is followed by what is in it: *the story of the night: the
most-liked photo, the most-liked message, what you decided, and every picture.*

## 3. The stranger test: the gate for every piece

Before anything is delivered, read it as someone who has never heard of After Stories and has
only this one piece: no bio, no site, no earlier video. They must be able to answer:

1. **What is it?** (an app for one event with your group)
2. **What does it do for me?** (the one proof in this piece)
3. **What do I do now?** (after-stories.com, or the App Store)

A piece that fails any of the three is not delivered. Write the three answers in the shotlist or
the post file, under `Stranger test:`, quoting the line in the piece that gives each answer.

## 4. The rules

1. **Say what it is, every time.** The sentence from §2.1, full or short, is in the body of every
   piece: on screen in a video, on a slide in a carousel, in the caption, at the first mention in
   a blog post. Not only in the bio or on the end card.
2. **One piece: one situation, one proof.** Not a tour. A second proof is a second piece.
3. **Open on a situation people recognise, in the words they would type.** The opening is a
   moment one of the three proofs answers (§2.3, column 3). A mood, a season or a pretty table
   that no proof answers is not an opening. `marketing.md` › Hook doctrine still holds: no
   feature claim at frame 0.
4. **Show the product.** The proof is shown in the real app (the recordings in
   `/nas/spark/footage/after-stories/app/`, or App Store screenshots). AI footage sets the scene;
   it never stands in for the app. A video where the app is never on screen fails the stranger
   test.
5. **Every line stands alone.** Read each line by itself. *"One stream for the whole event."*
   means nothing to a stranger; *"Everyone's photos land in the event."* does. Site lines are not
   safe to lift without their page around them.
6. **Concrete, not abstract.** Name the thing: the address, Saturday, who's driving, the photo
   from the rooftop. Use §5's words.
7. **The same word for the same thing**, every time (§5). Variation reads as a different product.
8. **Deleting always comes after saving**, in the same breath (`positioning.md` › What the
   48-hour deletion means). Never fear, never loss.
9. **Say the platform plainly.** An iPhone app. Friends without an iPhone join in their browser
   from the invite link. Never promise an Android app.
10. **End on the next step**, the same every time: the icon and `after-stories.com` on the end
    card (Robert, 2026-10-29), and the channel's link line as the caption's last line
    (`marketing.md` §2).
11. **Only facts from §8.** No invented numbers, quotes, reviews, user counts or dates. No
    competitor by name. **Nothing about price**, not even "free" (*Robert*, §11).
12. **Clear first, then funny.** Warm and witty, cheeky, never laddish; the office lane is
    calmer. The joke lives in the situation. The explanation is never a joke.

## 6. Who we talk to: three lanes

In every lane the person we talk to is **the one who ends up organising**. The sentence and the
proofs are the same; the situation and the examples change. A piece is in exactly one lane.

| Lane | Who | Their situation, in their words | Examples in the sentence | Lead proof | Register |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Friends** (primary, at least half of all pieces) | The friend who always ends up planning | "which weekend did we land on?" · "can someone send the pics??" | a dinner, a party, a weekend away | 2, then 1, then 3 | Warm, witty |
| **Parents** | The parent who ends up making the car list | "who's driving on Saturday?" · "is there a seat for Emil?" | the kids' match, a cup, an away game | 1 as the car list, then 2 | Practical, kind |
| **Colleagues** | The one who gets handed the office party | "see the mail thread" · "can everyone send me their photos?" | the team dinner, the Christmas party | 1, then 3 (no work chat left behind) | Calm. No nightlife or hangover jokes |

**The parents' sentence** (the carpool is their proof 1): *After Stories is an app for the kids'
match day. Every parent sees who drives which child, out and home, and everyone gets the photos
from the game.* The children never use the app (18+): a parent types a first name. Never show or
name a real child.

## 7. How to build each format

**Short video, Reel, TikTok** (formats and lengths per `marketing.md` §5):

| Beat | What | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Situation | The opening line, from §2.3 column 3 or the lane's situations | In their words; no product fact |
| 2. What it is | The short sentence on screen (spoken in full if there is a voice) | Before the halfway mark |
| 3. The proof | The real app doing the one proof, with its line from §2.3 | At least 2 seconds of the app, readable |
| 4. End card | The icon and `after-stories.com` | Robert, 2026-10-29 |

Caption: line 1 the situation as a question; line 2 the **full** sentence; the last line the
channel's link line (`marketing.md` §2); then the hashtags. The proof's line is on screen, so the
caption doesn't repeat it.

**Carousel / photo post:** slide 1 the situation; 2–3 slides the viewer nods at (no product
fact); then one slide with the short sentence over a real app frame; the last slide the proof's
line and `after-stories.com`.

**Blog post:** the article still helps on its own, with at most two mentions (`prompt.md` §4).
The first mention is the full sentence. The mention uses the one proof that answers the
article's problem. The FAQ has *"What is After Stories?"*, answered with the full sentence plus
the platform line (rule 9), because FAQ answers are quoted without the article around them.

**Paid ad:** an ad may be an ad. The short sentence is on screen within 3 seconds; then one
proof; then the App Store.

**Profile bio:** *The app for one event with your group. Plan it together. Everyone gets the
photos.* Then the link.

**Answering "what is this?"** in a comment: the full sentence, one proof, the link. Nothing else.

## 8. The facts (checked 2026-11-05)

Checked against the live App Store description (version 1.3), the app's shipped modules and
`docs/product.md` › Build order in the app repo. **When a new app version goes live, re-check
this section against the live App Store description the same day; where they disagree, the store
wins and this section is corrected.** Every other file points here instead of keeping its own
list.

**True today, and safe to say**

- An **iPhone app**, iOS 26 or later, on the App Store (app 6815212070).
- **Friends without an iPhone join in their browser** from the invite link: they chat, vote and
  share photos. Creating an event, the morning-after summary and saving all photos at once need
  the iPhone app.
- **An event needs only a name.** It starts from a template: *Plan together*, *Party*,
  *Game day*, *Free form*.
- **Invite** with a link, a code or a QR code.
- **Vote in the chat**: which day, where to eat, which film. What's settled moves to **Event
  details at the top**: the time, the place, who brings what. A date can be added to the calendar.
- **A place** is a venue with its address and a Maps link. Never anyone's location.
- **One evening or several days** (since 1.3): a weekend away or a festival is one event, with
  what was decided shown day by day.
- **During the event**: one chat with messages, photos and places; one tap to like.
- **The morning after**, everyone gets the summary: the most-liked photo, the most-liked message,
  what you decided, a few numbers. **Save all photos** to the phone with one tap.
- **Game day**: every parent sees who drives which child, out and home; anyone can move a child.
- **Private**: only the people invited can see an event. No feed, no followers, no strangers, no
  ads. Photos are stripped of location and camera data on the phone, before upload.
- **Deleted for everyone 48 hours after the event ends**, with a reminder first.
- **Up to 50 people** per event.
- Sign in with Apple. The account can be deleted in the app.

**Not true today: never claim**

- RSVP ("are you coming"), a gift list, an invitation text, a program, the match score, *repeat
  for the next game*. They are built but not released.
- An Android app. Creating an event in the browser.
- Sharing the summary outside the event, a public page, a feed.
- Live location, a map of where people are.
- More than 50 people (1.4 raises it for some templates; not before 1.4 is live). Weddings and
  public events.
- That photos are kept or backed up after the 48 hours.

**True, but not for marketing without Robert's word**

- **Price.** The app is free to use, and photo quality is the one paid thing (*After Stories
  Pro*, the *Event Pass*). The rule is still: say nothing about price (§11).
- The number of languages in the app (the site is in five).
- Partner templates and unlock codes (1.4, in review).
