The bill arrives, and the table goes quiet in a specific way. Nobody asks, *so how are we doing this?* Everyone is just looking at their phone and doing the arithmetic alone, the way you do arithmetic when you don't want to be wrong in front of the group. The split, at a table of eight, is the one part of the night that nobody planned.

The money format usually gets decided on the spot, with everyone tired and the food still warm, which is a bad moment to decide anything. The fix is to move the decision to the moment you are planning the night, when it's a sentence in an invite rather than a calculation nobody wants to do.

## The two honest formats

The first is the **even split**: the shared bill divides evenly between everyone at the table. It is the right pick when the group is ordering about the same and the night is the same length for everyone. The second is **separate tabs**: each person pays their own food and drinks, and the group never does a shared division at all. It is the right pick when one person is having a long night, a couple are skipping a round, or the food varies a lot from plate to plate.

There is no universal right answer. The format depends on the group, the venue, and how long someone is staying, which is why it has to be a decision the group makes rather than a guess.

## How to handle the shared bits

Even in a separate-tabs night, the bill is rarely purely per person. There is the shared bottle, the appetizer passed around the table, and the tip that belongs to everyone. Handle those before the arithmetic, or the arithmetic has three extra moving parts.

Shared dishes split between the people who shared them, not between the whole table. If two of you split the truffle pasta and the rest had mains, the pasta is between those two. Everyone's own drinks stay on their own tab. The tip is its own line. Agree whether it splits evenly or in proportion to each tab, and put that in the invite with the format. That is the shape that keeps the math from turning into a second conversation.

## Put the format in the invite

The money format belongs in the invite with the date and the place. The invite is where the group reads the plan once, and it's the last easy moment to raise money at all. So the invite carries it in plain words: *We are splitting the shared bill*, or *it's each on their own tab*. One line.

At a birthday dinner the invite also says whose share the group is covering, the same money step from the [birthday checklist](/blog/birthday-party-logistics/). The group doesn't need a debate, because the choice is small and the person writing the invite has already made a sensible call.

If someone wants a different format, they say so in the planning, not when the bill is in front of them. We wrote about how the invite should carry the plan in [why plans die in the group chat](/blog/group-chat-plans/).

## Handle the one person who stays longer

This is the case that breaks the split. Everyone is ordering about the same, and then one person stays for a fourth drink while the rest leave. If you wait until the end to settle the shared bill, the extra rounds ride on everyone's share, and one person ends up minding it.

The clean move is to let the late drink settle on its own. The person staying orders the extra rounds on their own tab, or the organizer writes them down. Then the shared bill splits as the others leave, and the one staying pays the later rounds before they go. Say it plainly, and it's a ten-second thing, easier than folding a fourth round into a split of eight after the fact.

## A worked example

A table of eight, a dinner that comes to about 350, and one person staying for a fourth round. It is made up, and it is only there to show the shape.

With an even split the whole bill divides evenly between the eight people at the table. Each person sends the organizer their share, whoever is paying at the till collects the card, and that is the whole arithmetic.

With separate tabs the same dinner works differently. The food comes to about 240, and each person pays their own share of it. Two people split a shared bottle of wine, so that 60 goes between those two, not between all eight. The tip is agreed up front: an extra 32, split evenly as its own line, so another 4 a head.

One person stays for a 16 round that is theirs alone. The organizer works it out while the food is warm and sends each person one number; the rest pay them back with a transfer in the same chat. Nobody does this at the table.

## How it looks when the money is settled

It is the dinner where the bill arrives and nobody has to ask, because the answer was in the invite. If it is the even split, the shared part divides and it is done before the food goes cold. If it is separate tabs, the group already knew and the venue already could. The one person who stayed for a last drink settled their own rounds, and the evening ends on the night.

The one thing to do differently: put the money format in the invite, in plain words, before anyone orders. That is the whole move. Everything else is arithmetic.
